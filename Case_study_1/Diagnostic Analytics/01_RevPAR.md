# Why Did RevPAR Increase Only Slightly in 2025?

> **Note on figures:** Absolute ADR/RevPAR dollar values below reflect a known gap between the reservations and room-inventory data sources (FACT_RESERVATIONS vs. FACT_ROOM_INVENTORY), which understates true dollar levels by a roughly constant factor across all three years. **Year-over-year % changes are reliable and are the basis for every conclusion in this report; the absolute dollar figures should not be read as real portfolio rates.**

Based on the descriptive analysis, RevPAR increased from **$15.47 in 2023** to **$16.11 in 2024 (+4.17%)**, before slowing to **$16.22 in 2025 (+0.68%)**.

| Year | ADR    | RevPAR | Occupancy |
| ---- | ------ | ------ | --------- |
| 2023 | $22.56 | $15.47 | ~68.6%    |
| 2024 | $23.49 | $16.11 | ~68.6%    |
| 2025 | $23.63 | $16.22 | ~68.6%    |

Since:

RevPAR = ADR × Occupancy

I investigated both components separately to determine the primary driver of RevPAR performance.

## 1. Occupancy Held Effectively Flat

Occupancy stayed close to **68.6% across all three years**, with a slight uptick in 2025.

This indicates demand was stable rather than a primary growth driver. The small 2025 uptick accounts for a minor residual — part of why 2025's RevPAR growth (0.68%) came in slightly above ADR growth alone (0.61%) — but the effect is small relative to ADR's contribution.

**Key Insight:**
Demand was effectively flat. Occupancy is not the primary explanation for the change in RevPAR, though it contributed a small residual in 2025.

---

### 2. ADR Growth Slowed Significantly

ADR increased as follows:

- **$22.56 (2023) → $23.49 (2024)** (**+4.12%**)
- **$23.49 (2024) → $23.63 (2025)** (**+0.61%**)

The ADR growth rate in 2025 closely mirrors the RevPAR growth rate of **0.68%**, confirming ADR — not occupancy — as the dominant driver of RevPAR movement in both years.

**Key Insight:**
The slowdown in ADR growth is the primary explanation for slower RevPAR growth in 2025.

---

### 3. Channel Analysis: A Mixed Picture, Not Uniform Stagnation

The full 8-channel breakdown shows most channels actually grew ADR in 2025 — the deceleration is concentrated in a specific, narrower cluster than a first pass suggested.

| Channel   | Type         | 2024 ADR | 2025 ADR | YoY Change |
| --------- | ------------ | -------- | -------- | ---------- |
| Channel 1 | Corporate    | 380.08   | 388.08   | **+2.10%** |
| Channel 2 | Travel Agent | 381.44   | 386.35   | **+1.29%** |
| Channel 3 | Third-Party  | 383.72   | 386.76   | **+0.79%** |
| Channel 4 | Corporate    | 384.83   | 386.98   | **+0.56%** |
| Channel 5 | App          | 381.44   | 382.11   | **+0.18%** |
| Channel 6 | Direct       | 384.04   | 381.71   | **-0.61%** |
| Channel 7 | Third-Party  | 386.39   | 383.18   | **-0.83%** |
| Channel 8 | Website      | 385.76   | 382.28   | **-0.90%** |

Five of eight channels grew ADR in 2025, led by Channel 1 (+2.10%). Only three declined: Channel 6, Channel 7, and Channel 8 — all in a tight -0.6% to -0.9% band. Notably, Channel 8 (Website) and Channel 5 (App) — two access points for the same underlying booking source — moved in **opposite directions** (-0.90% vs +0.18%), which points more toward a channel-specific rate-setting or parity inconsistency than a demand-side or market-wide explanation.

**Key Insight:**
ADR softness in 2025 wasn't broad-based — it was a specific decline in three channels (Channel 6, Channel 7, Channel 8) offsetting growth in the other five, including a strong Channel 1 showing. The Website/App divergence (Channel 8 vs Channel 5) is a distinct, investigable finding on its own.

---

## Business Insight

The RevPAR slowdown in 2025 was **not a demand issue** — occupancy held effectively flat at ~68.6%. It was a **pricing issue concentrated in three specific channels**, not a portfolio-wide plateau.

Five of eight channels grew ADR in 2025; the deceleration in the portfolio-level number is attributable to declines in Channel 6, Channel 7, and Channel 8 pulling against otherwise positive momentum elsewhere — most notably a strong +2.10% gain on Channel 1.

**Business Case: Three Channels Are Dragging an Otherwise Positive ADR Story**

Occupancy is stable, so ADR remains the portfolio's primary growth lever — and on five of eight channels, that lever is working. The exceptions are worth investigating individually rather than as a single "pricing plateau":

- **Channel 6 (Direct) and Channel 8 (Website)** are both channels the hotel can adjust rate rules on directly, without a third-party negotiation — the fastest place to start.
- **Channel 8 vs. Channel 5 diverging** (-0.90% vs +0.18%) on what's presumably the same underlying inventory and source is the most specific, actionable lead here — it suggests a rate-parity or configuration gap between the website and app rather than a market condition.
- **Channel 7 declining while Channel 3 grew** (+0.79%) — both third-party channels — suggests the softness isn't "third-party channels" broadly either; it's specific to one partner relationship.

**Recommendation:** Start with a rate-parity check between the Website (Channel 8) and App (Channel 5) — that divergence is the clearest signal of a fixable, internal cause rather than external market pressure. Follow with a Direct-channel (Channel 6) pricing-rule audit and a review of Channel 7's specific rate loading versus Channel 3.

---

## Executive Summary

RevPAR growth decelerated from **4.17% in 2024** to **0.68% in 2025**, driven by a slowdown in ADR growth (4.12% → 0.61%) while occupancy stayed effectively flat (~68.6%). The full channel breakdown shows this was not a portfolio-wide pricing plateau: five of eight channels grew ADR in 2025, led by Channel 1 (+2.10%). The deceleration is concentrated in three channels — Channel 6, Channel 7, and Channel 8 — with the Website vs. App divergence (Channel 8: -0.90%, Channel 5: +0.18%) standing out as the most specific and actionable lead. This points to a targeted rate-governance issue in a small set of channels rather than a demand or market-wide condition.

_(Absolute dollar figures reflect a known data-source gap and should be read as directional, not as audited rate figures — see note at top.)_

## My recommendation:

→ Direct channel: audit for over-discounting. Is the property selling meaningfully below its stated rate? How often are reservations getting declined? Worth tracing back through reservations and central ops.

→ Third-party channel: review rate parity and profitability. Although the ADR decline is relatively small, third-party bookings carry commission costs that can further reduce net revenue. Validating pricing strategy and channel profitability will help determine whether corrective action is required.

→ Website: start with the website-vs-app rate divergence — that's the clearest, most fixable lead here. Then check pricing against the loyalty rate, and confirm the booking flow itself is functioning properly, not just mispriced.
